Phase 06 · 8–12 hours + firing

Surface & voice.

Surface is a layered event: clay, timing, tool, material, firing, and light. Replace random decoration with a testable visual language.

Learn to read surfaceSee the resolved vessel

Aim

Build a surface vocabulary from evidence.

Prerequisite

Reliable forms and joins; access to studio-approved slips, underglazes, glazes, and firing.

By the end

You can analyze surfaces, time marks to clay state, run a test matrix, and adapt a motif to volume.

Deliverables

A surface reading, timing lab, 12-tile matrix, form translation, and one resolved vessel.

Material and application gateFor beginners, use studio-approved wet, brush-applied products unless locally trained for another method. Read the label and SDS; verify cone range, atmosphere, clay-body compatibility, application amount, drying, and cleanup. Do not dry-mix or spray materials, blow dust from ware, or casually dry-sand. Spraying requires an authorized compliant booth. Keep glaze off feet, lid contacts, and kiln-contact surfaces by the studio’s required clearance. A firing cookie is secondary containment, not permission to use an untested runny glaze. Two approved products do not make their layered combination approved: test the exact body, order, thickness, and firing. “Two layers or operations” may mean texture plus slip or carving plus underglaze; it does not require stacked glazes.
Just-in-time firing modelBisque firing makes ware easier to handle but does not establish final glaze fit. In glaze firing, materials melt and interact according to heatwork, atmosphere, application, clay body, and location. Phase 7 develops this model in depth.
06.1

Read before you decorate.

What exactly makes a surface feel quiet, dense, directional, or unstable?

Structure

Field, border, focal point, rhythm, scale, density, and negative space.

Material evidence

Cut, impress, trail, brush, resist, scrape, pool, break, and expose.

Relationship to form

Follow, oppose, frame, interrupt, conceal, or reveal the volume.

Study · Three surface readings60 min

Choose three ceramic works from a museum collection. For each, make a grayscale thumbnail and annotate: visual route, densest zone, quietest zone, likely sequence of layers, and how the pattern responds to the form. Separate direct evidence from inference.

06.2

Timing changes the mark.

Lab · Timing + technique90 min

Prepare small tiles from the same clay. At workable, soft leather-hard, and firm leather-hard states, test impressing, carving, and brushing an approved slip or underglaze. Keep tool, gesture, and size constant.

Record edge crispness, drag, distortion, flaking, absorption, and repairability. Do not casually sand dry clay; use wet cleanup and your studio’s silica controls.

Lab · 12-tile layer matrix

VariableLevelsHold constant
GroundBare clay / approved slipClay body and tile size
MarkIncised / brushed / resistedMotif and scale
Top layerNone / studio glazeApplication method and firing

Number every tile on the back. Record material names, batch if known, layer order, application count or measured mass where practical, firing identification, and result.

Never infer functional safety from a label or gloss alone.Suitability depends on the exact clay–glaze–application–firing system, surface condition, intended use, studio guidance, and sometimes testing.
06.3

Translate pattern into volume.

Three maps

Contour · use · attention

Map where the form turns, where hands and lips meet it, and where the eye should pause. A surface succeeds when these maps converse.

Distortion is information

A motif viewed on a flat sketch will compress around a shoulder and accelerate around a narrow neck. Use paper wraps, acetate overlays, or a sacrificial clay maquette to predict that change.

Study · One motif, three translations2 hours
  1. Create a simple two-part motif and a clear negative-space rule.
  2. Apply it to three small forms by following contour, opposing contour, and concentrating at a transition.
  3. Photograph each from four fixed viewpoints. Identify which version best clarifies the form and why.
06.4

Project: surface as structure.

Integrated project

One vessel, one visual rule, no filler.

Choose a successful tile result and translate it to a handbuilt vessel. Use at least two layers or operations, one deliberate quiet zone, and a surface decision at a major form transition.

  1. Write a one-sentence surface intention and make a full-wrap paper study.
  2. Make a test fragment with the same curve and layer sequence as the vessel.
  3. Apply each layer at the clay state supported by your timing lab.
  4. After firing, compare test, intention, and result under raking and diffuse light.
  5. Write what the kiln amplified, muted, blurred, or changed.

Inspiration · look comparatively

Surface can move, carve, and pool.

Compare repetition painted over volume, slip cut away to expose the body, and glaze redirected by gravity.
